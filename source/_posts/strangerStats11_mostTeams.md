Remember when Jock Landale got traded twice in 24 hours and [drove a truck across the country](/2026/02/09/strangerStats06_careerHighInDebut/) before dropping a career high? That was a one-day bannerman story.

Dennis Schröder's whole career is the bannerman story.

The Cavaliers just shipped Schröder to the Charlotte Hornets for Tre Mann. It's the **ninth time** Schröder has been traded in his career — the second-most in NBA history, behind only Trevor Ariza at 11. Charlotte is his **12th NBA team**, his sixth stop in the last 20 months, and he's about to play for his 15th head coach. Schröder is 32 years old. Somewhere, a moving company has him on a retainer.

That got me wondering the obvious stranger-stats question: is 12 teams the most? And more importantly — who has actually **played a game** for the most teams?

### By the Numbers

The answer to the first question is no. **Ish Smith played for 13 teams.** Schröder is one team away from tying the record — if he checks in for Charlotte (or whoever's next), he joins Smith at the top.

The second question is the fun one, because "on a team" is a paperwork stat and "played a game" is a box-score stat. I went through **1.67 million player-game rows** (1946-2026, regular season + playoffs) and counted the distinct franchises each player actually logged minutes for:

- **13 teams:** Ish Smith (the record)
- **12 teams:** Chucky Brown, Jim Jackson, Tony Massenburg, Joe Smith, Garrett Temple
- **11 teams:** Jeff Green (still active), Anthony Tolliver, Mike James
- Being traded a lot doesn't mean you played a lot: Trevor Ariza is the all-time trade king at **11 trades** but "only" played for **10 teams**.

Ish Smith is the GOAT. **13 franchises, 747 games** — he averaged roughly 57 games per stop. The man basically rented jerseys by the month. He never averaged double-digit points for a season, never made an All-Star team, and still carved out a 15-year career by being exactly what every team needed: a point guard who could get the ball over half court and not die.

The rest of the club is a hall of fame of "wait, he played THERE too?" Joe Smith is the wildest resume — he was the **No. 1 overall pick in 1995** and ended up playing for 12 teams anyway, with 1,059 career games. Jim Jackson was a 20-point scorer in Dallas who still couldn't find a permanent address. Tony Massenburg played 12 teams in 15 seasons and won a ring in the middle of it all. Garrett Temple just retired this summer to take an assistant coach job with the Mavericks — his 12th team was also his last.

### What We Learned

The bannerman record is about games, not jerseys. Ish Smith played for 13 teams and is untouchable at the top — for now. Schröder is one stop away from tying him, and at 32 with a tradable expiring contract, that's closer than it sounds. He's also two trades away from tying Ariza's trade record. Dennis Schröder might end up owning both records before this is over.

And the real lesson: if you're a backup point guard who can dribble and defend, you never have to unpack. Trevor Ariza did it with 3-and-D wings. Ish Smith did it with speed. Schröder is doing it with a passport.
