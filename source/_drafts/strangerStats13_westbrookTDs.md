Russell Westbrook retired this year after 18 seasons. He finished with **209 regular-season triple-doubles**, which is the most in NBA history by a margin so big it stops being a record and starts being a separate category of existence.

Oscar Robertson had 181. Magic Johnson had 139. Jason Kidd had 107. Westbrook lapped all of them and kept going.

Here's the thing about 209 that doesn't get said enough: it's not just more than any player ever. It's more than almost every **team's entire history**. Let's put it in the ridiculous contexts it deserves.

### Westbrook vs. Every Franchise

{% htmlblock p13/every_franchise_s_all_time_triple_doubles_vs_westbrook_s_209 %}

Westbrook alone recorded more triple-doubles than **27 of the 30 current franchises** in their entire existence. The only three above him:

- **Lakers (280)** — Magic, West, Kobe, LeBron, all of them, combined
- **Nuggets (245)** — that's Jokić, basically
- **Kings (240)** — and a huge chunk of that is Oscar Robertson in Cincinnati, in a different era, in a different sport

The Thunder, the franchise he spent a decade with, have **201 total**. Westbrook alone is 138 of them. Take his games out of Oklahoma City's history and the franchise drops to 63. He didn't just set the franchise record — he was the franchise record.

### Westbrook vs. the Hall of Fame Point Guards

Oscar Robertson: 117. Magic Johnson: 139. Jason Kidd: 107. Steve Nash: 3. John Stockton: 0. Westbrook has more than all of them individually, and he's 209 of the 449 combined career triple-doubles recorded by 15 Hall of Fame point guards in this dataset. Fifteen of the greatest point guards who ever lived, and Westbrook is nearly half of them combined.

### Westbrook vs. the One-and-Done Club

There are **198 players** in NBA history who recorded exactly **one** career triple-double. One hundred ninety-eight different guys who got there once, saw the view, and never came back. Their entire combined output: 198 triple-doubles.

Westbrook alone: 209. The one-and-done club, combined, couldn't catch a guy who treated triple-doubles like a recurring subscription.

### Westbrook vs. His Era

During his 18-year career, the entire NBA recorded **1,538** regular-season triple-doubles. Westbrook has 13.6% of all of them — including **113 dates where he was the only triple-double in the league that day**. Fifty-four percent of the time he triple-doubled, nobody else in the NBA did that night.

He also had **14 triple-doubles in April 2021 alone** — nearly as many as the entire NBA of the 1950s produced (16), in one month, while playing for the Wizards.

And the active league? Nikola Jokić is the active leader at **197** — 12 behind, still playing, and suddenly the record chase has a second act. LeBron sits at 125. Luka at 90. The entire active league combined for 1,098 career triple-doubles. Westbrook's 209 is almost a fifth of that.

### The Road to 209

{% htmlblock p13/the_road_to_209 %}

{% htmlblock p13/triple_doubles_per_82_games_by_age %}

His first triple-double came March 2, 2009, as a 20-year-old. Then it took him **2,163 days** to get to No. 10 — six seasons for the first ten. From there the dam broke: 10 to 50 took 683 days, 50 to 100 took 451, and he hit **No. 182 on May 10, 2021**, passing Oscar Robertson's record that had stood for 47 years. His final 28 took another 1,769 days — the slowdown of age, and also of playing for teams that stopped running through him.

The pace numbers are insane: 11 consecutive games with a triple-double (2019), 14 in a single month (April 2021), and a peak of **37.1 triple-doubles per 82 games at age 29** — more than half the season, every season, at his best. He was 24 once and recorded zero. Then he was 28, 29, 31 and 33 and made up for lost time.

### The Victims

{% htmlblock p13/westbrook_s_triple_double_victims_top_14 %}

The Jazz saw 10 Westbrook triple-doubles and won exactly one of them. Orlando went 2-8. Indiana 3-8. James Harden, his old running mate turned rival, was on the floor for **11** of them — nobody in the league watched more Westbrook triple-doubles go in. Home and road barely mattered: 114 at home, 95 on the road. He never recorded more than two consecutive triple-doubles against the same opponent, because the schedule wouldn't let him.

### What We Learned

Westbrook didn't just break the triple-double record. He out-triple-doubled 27 entire franchises, out-triple-doubled the one-and-done club, out-triple-doubled every Hall of Fame point guard who ever lived, and did it while the rest of the league was averaging maybe six a year.

The record's second act is already running. Jokić is 12 away at 197, and he plays a style that makes triple-doubles look like cardio. But whatever Jokić does, Westbrook's number will always be the one that made the stat feel broken.
