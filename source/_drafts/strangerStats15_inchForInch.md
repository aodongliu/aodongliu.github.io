The fantasy leaderboard has a size problem, and it shows up the moment you rank everything.

Rank every NBA game since 1977-78 by raw FanDuel and the all-time top 20 is basically a big-man list. **13 of the 20 best games ever** belong to players listed 6'11" or taller, and the other seven to players between 6'5" and 6'10". Not one spot goes to a guard under 6'5". The greatest raw game ever played by a sub-6'5" player, **Donovan Mitchell's 71-point night** in 2023, ranks just **30th** all time. A center's ordinary 25-and-15 with a few blocks is worth more than a guard's career night, because the center's box score is simply bigger. Inches do that.

The UFC solved this problem with weight classes. Nobody asks whether a flyweight could beat a heavyweight; the sport splits by size, crowns a champion in each division, and then asks a separate, cleaner question: pretend everyone weighed the same, who's the best? That's pound-for-pound.

Basketball never built the equivalent. So let's build it. Six height classes, a game king and a season king in each one, and then the pound-for-pound question, which for basketball means inch for inch.

**FanDuel = PTS + 1.2×REB + 1.5×AST + 3×STL + 3×BLK − TOV**

## The Six Height Classes

- **6'0" and under** — 167 players in the data
- **6'1" to 6'4"** — 918 players
- **6'5" to 6'7"** — 1,155 players
- **6'8" to 6'10"** — 1,140 players
- **6'11" to 7'2"** — 501 players
- **7'3" and up** — 28 players. That's not a typo. In the whole history of the dataset, only 28 players were listed at 7'3" or taller, and they account for just **2,681 games of 30+ minutes**, against 116,767 for the 6'5"-6'7" class. The top class is a ghost town, which matters for the rankings.

A few things worth reading off those boards:

- The shortest class's ceiling is **Allen Iverson's 60-point night** in 2005 (88.8 FanDuel). It takes sixty points for a 6-footer to reach what the tall classes get from an ordinary superstar night, and it's the only sub-6'5" game anywhere near the all-time raw top 20. The season king is **Chris Paul's 2008-09** (50.9 per game), the 22.5-point, 11-assist season that almost won him MVP at 6'0".
- **Donovan Mitchell's 71** tops the 6'1"-6'4" class (96.1), and since it's the best raw game by anyone under 6'5", it doubles as the class's proof that the raw leaderboard is rigged: it ranks 30th all time.
- **Michael Jordan's 69-point night** in Cleveland owns the 6'5"-6'7" class (112.6), the second-best raw game ever played. Kobe's 81 is second in class (100.2). Jordan also owns four of the class's top five seasons, topped by 1988-89 at 61.5 FanDuel per game.
- The 6'8"-6'10" title race is the closest in any class. **Bam Adebayo's 83-point game** this past March scored 105.3 FanDuel, and it beat **Luka Doncic's 60/21/10 triple-double** by **0.1 points** (105.2). The season crown goes to Luka's 2023-24 at 60.4 per game.
- The 6'11"-7'2" class is the strongest class in history, and its king is the king of everything. **Hakeem Olajuwon's 1987 masterpiece** (38 points, 17 rebounds, 12 blocks, 7 steals) is the best raw game ever played at 120.4 FanDuel, and Hakeem holds three of the class's top five. **Nikola Jokic's 2024-25** (63.3 per game) is the best raw season in history, too.
- The towers' game king is **Ralph Sampson's 13-block night** in 1983 (28-18-5 for 90.1 FanDuel), and their season king is **Victor Wembanyama's 2024-25**, 54.6 FanDuel per game across 46 games before his season was cut short. The 7'3"+ class is the only class whose champions trail the class below them, because at that size the players are rare enough that the all-time greats are the only ones who ever produce.

## Inch for Inch

The class champions settle who's best *within* a size. They don't settle who's best, period, and they shouldn't: Jokic's 63.3-per-game season beats Chris Paul's 50.9, but a good chunk of that gap is the 11 inches between them doing box-score work.

So the pound-for-pound question divides production by height: **FanDuel ÷ inches**. Jordan's 69-point game: 112.6 ÷ 78 inches = **1.444 per inch**. Wilt's 100 doesn't get to play, steals, blocks, and turnovers weren't tracked in 1962, so the ranking starts in **1977-78**, the first season with a complete box score.

The greatest game ever played, inch for inch, is **Michael Jordan's 69 in Cleveland**: 69 points, 18 rebounds, 6 assists, 4 steals, from a 6'6" frame. And the closest challenger is Hakeem's 1987 game again, at 1.433. Hakeem produced more raw FanDuel than Jordan, 120.4 to 112.6, but he had to spread it across 84 inches, so Jordan won by a fraction of an inch. Literally.

Look at how the inch-for-inch list reorders the class champions. Mitchell's 71 jumps from the 30th-best raw game to **6th** per inch. Bam's 83 sits 5th, Kobe's 81 7th. An 81-point night and an 83-point night both lose to a 69-point night, and it's not the scoring that does it. It's the height. The tallest man in the top ten is Shaq, whose 24-point, 28-rebound, 15-block game is 9th: his blocks were worth 45 FanDuel points, more than his 24 points, and even that was barely enough at 7'1".

{% htmlblock p15/top10_seasons %}

The best season ever, inch for inch, is **Russell Westbrook's 2016-17 MVP year**: 31.9 points, 10.7 rebounds, 10.4 assists per game, 61.1 FanDuel per game from a 6'4" frame, for **0.804 per inch**. Jordan owns four of the top six seasons, and notice what beats the 37-point 1986-87: the all-around seasons, 1988-89 and 1987-88, the ones where he did everything. And the tallest player in the top five is **Nikola Jokic's 2024-25**: 63.3 FanDuel per game, the highest raw production of anyone on the list. Jokic produced more per game than Westbrook or Jordan. He just had to do it with 83 inches instead of 76. A 7-footer forcing his way into the per-inch club is the rarest thing on this list.

## What the Classes Did

The height classes give the raw leaderboard back to the little guys in a way it never had before. Chris Paul's 2008-09 is a real title. Iverson's 60 is a real title. Neither will ever appear on a raw all-time list next to a center's 20-and-20 with blocks, because on that list the center's inches are doing the scoring. In a class system, the small guard finally fights his own weight class, and he wins it.

Then the inch-for-inch list does what pound-for-pound was always supposed to do: it asks the unfair question across classes, knowing it's unfair. A 7-footer who produces 80% as much per inch is still worth more than a 6-footer who produces 120%, his inches protect the rim, warp the spacing, and change what the other team can even run. The per-inch list can't see any of that, which is exactly why the tallest player isn't always the best player, and why teams pay a premium for inches anyway.

What the list *can* see is who squeezed the most basketball out of every inch they were given. The answers are the same as they were before this whole exercise: **Jordan's 69**, **Westbrook's 2016-17**, and a pair of centers, Hakeem and Jokic, who did so much per inch that they forced their way into a guards' club anyway.
