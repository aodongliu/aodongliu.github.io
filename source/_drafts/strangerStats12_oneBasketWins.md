Every winning box score has a guy at the bottom with 2 points and 5 minutes. Nobody talks about him. He's just there.

But flip the game: what if that 2 points was the exact difference between a win and a loss?

That's the stat I went digging for. **Games decided by one basket** — won by 1 or 2 points — where some player scored 3 points or fewer, and his points were the margin. Take them away and the win disappears. Literally: "without my points, we don't win."

Here's the rule. A team wins by 1-2 points, a player on that team scores 1-3 points, and his points are at least the winning margin. Remove his points and the game flips to a loss or a tie. The box score doesn't know if his basket was a buzzer-beater or a first-quarter layup. All it knows is the math: those points were the whole ballgame.

### By the Numbers

Since 1946, there have been **8,067** of these player-games across **5,021** one-basket wins. A third of them are the sharpest version — **3,459** where the player's points exactly equaled the margin, meaning one single basket (or free throw) was the entire difference.

- **522** of them happened in the playoffs, across 330 games.
- **310** times a guy scored exactly 1 point in a 1-point win. One free throw. That's the whole game.
- **2,267** games had at least two players on the winning team pulling this off. The margin was so tight the whole bench chipped in.

### The Cards

**#1 · Mark West — all-time king of necessary points**

{% htmlblock p12/1_mark_west_all_time_king_of_necessary_points %}

Mark West is the GOAT of this stat and I promise you've never heard of him. A 6'10" backup center from 1985-1996, he finished with 21 necessary-point games in his career, and in **13** of them his points were exactly the margin. Two points here, one point there, and a 13-year career built on being the quietest difference-maker alive.

**#2 · Magic Johnson — 48 minutes, 2 points, 1-point win**

{% htmlblock p12/2_magic_johnson_48_minutes_2_points_1_point_win %}

May 20, 1983, playoffs. Magic Johnson plays all 48 minutes... and scores TWO points. The Lakers beat the Spurs by one anyway. Two points in a full game is impressive enough on its own, but two points that also happen to be the entire margin of victory? That's a stranger stat.

**#3 · Horace Grant — 1 point in the Finals clincher**

{% htmlblock p12/3_horace_grant_1_point_in_the_finals_clincher %}

Game 6 of the 1993 Finals. Bulls vs Suns. Chicago wins 99-98 to clinch the championship, and the entire margin of the title was one point. Horace Grant played 33 minutes and scored exactly one. One point, one championship. Without it, the Bulls are going to a Game 7.

**#4 · Jordan Clarkson — 2026 NBA Finals, Game 4**

{% htmlblock p12/4_jordan_clarkson_2026_nba_finals_game_4 %}

The Knicks beat the Spurs 107-106 in Game 4 of this year's Finals, and Jordan Clarkson's 2 points in under 5 minutes were the margin. Mitchell Robinson did the exact same thing in the same game — 2 points, 13 minutes. Two guys on the same title-hopeful team whose baskets were the difference, and neither of them will ever get credit for it.

**#5 · Kobe Bryant — 2 points in his final season**

{% htmlblock p12/5_kobe_bryant_2_points_in_his_final_season %}

March 30, 2016. Kobe's farewell tour. The Lakers beat Miami 102-100, and Kobe's 2 points in 8 minutes were the exact difference. The greatest Laker of all time, quietly providing the entire margin with one basket. Mamba out... with 2 points.

**#6 · Chris Paul — 2 points, 28 minutes, 1-point win**

{% htmlblock p12/6_chris_paul_2_points_28_minutes_1_point_win %}

Chris Paul has done this twice in the last three years. Nov 1, 2023 with Golden State: 28 minutes, 2 points, Warriors win by 1. Then this season, at 40 years old, he scored 3 points in a 2-point Clippers win over the Pelicans. The Point God, reduced to a margin god.

### The Playoff Club

Doing it in the playoffs is different. The stakes are higher, the margin is everything, and the guy who scored 2 points in 42 minutes looks like a hero and a villain at the same time:

Bruce Bowen in the 2007 Finals — 42 minutes, 2 points, Spurs win by 1. Tristan Thompson in the 2015 Finals — 39 minutes, 2 points, Cavs win by 2. Otis Thorpe in the 1994 Finals. These are championship moments where the bottom of the box score was the top of the story.

### The One-Free-Throw Club

The purest version of the stat: exactly 1 point in a 1-point win. One free throw was the entire game:

{% htmlblock p12/the_one_free_throw_club_1_point_in_a_1_point_win %}

Jason Collins played 41 minutes and scored one free throw's worth of points, and the Nets needed every bit of it. Andre Iguodala, 31 minutes, one point, Warriors win by one. In a league of 120-point games, this is the opposite extreme.

### What We Learned

In a one-basket league, the bottom of the box score matters exactly as much as the top. Mark West built a 13-year career on 2-point margins. Magic Johnson played 48 minutes and scored 2 points in a playoff win. Horace Grant won a championship with one point. And this year, the Knicks won a title with a bench full of 2-point difference-makers.

The next time someone drops 40 in a win by 40, remember: the guy who scored 2 in a win by 1 was worth more.
