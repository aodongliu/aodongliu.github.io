Every time I watch the Thunder I lose track of the Williamses. **Jalen**, **Jaylin**, **Kenrich**. Three of them on one roster for four straight seasons now, none of them related, and the announcers just have to deal with it.

That got me wondering. If you add up every point by every player with the same last name, which name has scored the most in NBA history?

I'm counting names, not relatives. Jr. and III are dropped, so Jaren Jackson Jr. is a Jackson. Regular season only.

---

### Johnson and Williams

There are **2,864** last names in the box scores, and about 80% of them belong to exactly one player. Two names are far ahead of everyone else.

{% htmlblock p17/johnson_williams %}

The Johnsons win by about 30,000 points, with ten fewer players than the Williamses. One of every 50 points ever scored in the NBA was scored by a Johnson.

Neither name has a big star at the top. The highest-scoring Johnson is **Joe Johnson** with 20,407 points, not Magic. No Williams has reached 17,000. This is a volume business.

{% htmlblock p17/top_25_table %}

**James** is 18th, and LeBron is 75% of it. LeBron by himself would rank 36th.

---

### The Malones

Look at #11 again.

{% htmlblock p17/malone %}

Three players in NBA history have been named Malone, and all three were good. That is more points than the 22 Andersons, the 21 Thompsons or the 24 Thomases.

Their careers overlapped, and for two seasons Moses and Jeff were teammates in Washington. So when the Bullets played Karl's Jazz, all three were on the floor. One night in 1987 it went like this:

{% htmlblock p17/malone_100_game %}

**Exactly 100 points** by Malones in one game. Only one other name has ever scored 100 in a game, and Chamberlain did it alone.

Jeff later joined Karl in Utah, where the two of them once combined for 78.

---

### One-Man Names

At the other end, some names have had one player ever, and he was enough.

{% htmlblock p17/one_man_names_table %}

**Kareem Abdul-Jabbar** alone outscored all but 44 names in history. In this table, at least. He scored his first couple of seasons as Lew Alcindor, and I'm counting everyone under the name the record books use now.

---

### Who Wins Each Season?

Same question, one season at a time. Which name scored the most points each year?

{% htmlblock p17/season_titles_table %}

Johnson and Williams have won exactly half of the 72 seasons between them.

**Wilt Chamberlain** won five in a row by himself. In 1961-62 he scored 4,029 and the runner-up was the entire Jones family with 2,588.

The **Van Arsdale** twins, Dick and Tom, won twice. That's two actual brothers outscoring every Smith and Johnson in the league.

Then the Johnsons took over for **15 straight seasons**. In 1981-82 there were 16 different Johnsons in the league. In 1982-83 they scored 13,744 points, close to double the Williamses in second place.

And **James** won in 2005-06, which was LeBron plus the best year of Mike James's life.

---

### Decided by One Point

{% htmlblock p17/one_point_seasons_table %}

Two seasons came down to a single point. Whaaaaat????

The 1991-92 one is my favorite. The Johnsons' streak reached 15 by one point, and they only got there on a technicality. A Magic rookie named Brian Williams scored 437 points that season. He later changed his name to **Bison Dele**, so those points don't count for the Williamses here. Under the name he had at the time, the Williamses win by 436 and the Johnson streak ends at 14.

---

### Stat by Stat

It isn't just points.

{% htmlblock p17/stat_crowns_table %}

The Johnsons take points, rebounds and assists. The Williamses take steals, blocks and threes. After 80 years they are 70 games apart.

The Williamses are catching up, though. Fourteen of them played last season, twice as many as the Johnsons, and they outscored them by about 1,200. At that pace they take the all-time points lead in roughly 25 years.

And the Thunder's three Williamses are not even the record for one team.
