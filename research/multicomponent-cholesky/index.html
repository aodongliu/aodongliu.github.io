    Numerical algorithms / 2023

    Shrinking the memory that quantum chemistry needs

    Quantum calculations can spend enormous amounts of memory storing interactions between particles. This work adapts matrix factorization to electron–proton calculations and checks its accuracy and computational cost on water clusters.

    Publication

    Multicomponent Cholesky Decomposition: Application to Nuclear–Electronic Orbital Theory

    Aodong Liu, Tianyuan Zhang, Sharon Hammes-Schiffer, Xiaosong Li

    Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation
    19 (18), 6255–6262 (2023)

    Read the published paper

    DOI: 10.1021/acs.jctc.3c00686

    Overview

    The problem

    Quantum chemistry calculations store huge tables of interactions between pairs of particles. Cholesky decomposition is a standard way to compress them, but treating protons as quantum particles adds mixed electron–proton interactions that break the symmetry the standard approach relies on.

    The approach

    This work proposes several ways to extend Cholesky decomposition to calculations that treat electrons and protons quantum mechanically together, and analyzes the error that each part of the compression introduces.

    What it found

    Benchmarks on water clusters, up to 27 water molecules with all 54 protons treated quantum mechanically, show the accuracy and computational cost of the approach.

    Abstract

    The Cholesky decomposition technique is commonly used to reduce the memory requirement for storing two-particle repulsion integrals in quantum chemistry calculations that use atomic orbital bases. However, when quantum methods use multicomponent bases, such as nuclear–electronic orbitals, additional challenges are introduced due to asymmetric two-particle integrals. This work proposes several multicomponent Cholesky decomposition methods for calculations using nuclear–electronic orbital density functional theory. To analyze the errors in different Cholesky decomposition components, benchmark calculations using water clusters are carried out. The largest benchmark calculation is a water cluster (H₂O)₂₇ where all 54 protons are treated quantum mechanically. This study provides energetic and timing analyses to demonstrate the accuracy and performance of the proposed multicomponent Cholesky decomposition method.
